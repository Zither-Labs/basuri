# Choose a flute

      Before classical ragas: get a beginner-friendly bamboo flute you can cover with your fingers and that responds to soft air.

      Most beginner guides start you on a **C Natural middle** bansuri — comfortable length, easier embouchure than a large bass flute.
    
    
      ## Size and key

      
        **Smaller / higher** flutes need less air and shorter stretch — good for small hands. **Middle C** is the usual adult starter. Long bass flutes are hard for day-one embouchure.

        The stamped key (C, G, A…) is a Western pitch label. For Indian practice we treat your tonic as **Sa** and match the practice-bar drone — same idea as Singing.

      
    
    
      ## Six vs seven finger holes

      
        Folk and starter bansuris often have **six** finger holes. Concert Hindustani bansuris often add a **seventh**. Either works for first sound. South Indian *venu* (often eight holes) is a sister tradition; this room focuses on the side-blown bansuri / basuri family used across North India and Nepal.

      
    
    
      ## What to buy

      
        - Bamboo with a clean blowing-hole edge — not cracked.

        - Holes you can seal with finger pads.

        - Prefer a true side-blown flute over a toy fipple whistle if you want classical embouchure.

        - Budget is fine for month one — practice matters more than upgrading early.

      
    
    
      ## Care

      
        Wipe condensation after practice. Store dry. Oil bamboo if your maker recommends it.
