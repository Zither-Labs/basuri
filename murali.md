# The murali

      **Murali** (मुरली) is one of the beloved names for the bamboo flute in Sanskrit and bhakti poetry — especially the flute of Krishna. In everyday speech it overlaps with **bansuri** / **basuri**, but the word carries a story as much as a fingering chart.

    
    
      ## Krishna and the murali

      
        Temple art and scripture link the transverse bamboo flute with Krishna — Venugopal, Muralidhar (“bearer of the murali”), Bansilal. The sound that calls the gopis in Vrindavan is imagined as this flute. Devotional songs still praise that call more often than they teach embouchure.

        In Nepal and North India you will also hear *murli* / *murali* for bamboo flutes in folk contexts; sometimes the same word can mean other wind instruments, so ask what the speaker means.

      
    
    
      ## Venu, murali, and vamshi

      
        Devotional and Puranic lists sometimes describe Krishna’s flutes as more than one instrument — for example traditions that name a short **venu**, a longer **murali**, and a **vamshi** with different lengths and hole counts. Treat these as sacred typology and poetry first; modern teaching studios usually hand you a practical bansuri sized for your hands.

        **Venu** in South India also names the Carnatic bamboo flute (often eight holes, brighter response). **Bansuri** is the usual Hindustani concert / folk side-blown bamboo flute (six or seven holes).

      
    
    
      ## Murali vs the bansuri you buy

      
        
          - **Murali** — poetic / divine name; also used loosely for bamboo flutes in folk and bhajan settings.

          - **Bansuri / basuri** — the practice and concert instrument this room teaches: side-blown bamboo, open embouchure hole, finger holes.

          - **Fipple “toy” flutes** — whistle mouthpiece; easier first noise, less flexible for classical bends (meend) and tone color.

        
        If a shop labels a beginner bamboo flute “murali,” it is often still a bansuri-style tube. Judge by blowing hole and finger holes, not only the sticker.

      
    
    
      ## Why the empty bamboo matters

      Teachers and saints often say the flute sings because it is hollow — breath (prana) becomes tone (sur) only when nothing blocks the tube. For practice that is also practical advice: leaks, tension, and forced air block the song as surely as pith left inside bamboo.
    
    
      ## If you want to play “like the murali”

      Start with [first sound](first-sound.html) and long tones. The mythology inspires; the embouchure still has to be built. When your Sa is steady, simple bhajan phrases on a few swaras are a beautiful goal — clearer than rushing into full ragas.
