Anatomy of a side-blown basuri

Imagine a hollow bamboo tube. One end is plugged (cork). Near that plug is the mouth hole (embouchure) you blow across. Along the top face are six finger holes. Many concert flutes add a seventh pancham (tuning) hole offset to the side near the open foot.

  • Top / cork end — closed with cork or rubber so the air column starts at a fixed place.
  • Embouchure (mouth hole) — oval hole you split air across. Size and undercutting change response a lot.
  • Inner diameter (ID / bore) — hollow width inside. Bigger bore → bigger sound, more air, larger holes.
  • Wall thickness — bamboo wall. Affects hole “chimney” depth and how sharp edges feel.
  • Outer diameter (OD) — roughly ID + 2× wall. Your fingers wrap the OD.
  • Finger holes 1–6 — numbered from the lips toward the foot. Hole 1 is closest to the mouth hole.
  • Hole 7 / pancham — side hole that helps middle Pa speak cleanly; often drilled last and offset.
  • Foot / open end — open tip. Shortening it raises the all-closed pitch.

Side-blown modes (bamboo + PVC) use a cork or end-cap and a wall embouchure. Murali mode uses an end-blown duct — see modes and The murali.

How pitch works (beginner physics)

A bansuri behaves like an open tube: air vibrates from the embouchure to wherever the tube “ends” acoustically — the open foot, or the first open finger hole.

Rough rule (ideal open pipe):

sounding length ≈ speed_of_sound ÷ (2 × frequency)

Higher note → shorter sounding length → hole closer to the lips. That is why hole 1 (Ma) sits nearer the mouth than hole 6 (Dha).

What “key” means on a shop bansuri

  • C Natural medium (common beginner): three-hole Sa ≈ C5 (~523 Hz at A=440). All six closed, soft air ≈ G4 (lower Pa).
  • Bass charts: same letter names but roughly one octave lower (4th-octave Sa).
  • Indian colour names: White-1 = C, Black-1 = C♯, White-2 = D, … (Safed / Kali series).

Makers often cut length for Pa first (all holes closed), then drill upward: Dha → Ni → Sa → Re → Ga → Ma, enlarging as they listen. The calculator prints finished mark positions so you can mark all at once — still enlarge gradually.

Glossary

  • Labium / window — cutting edge of a murali / recorder fipple where the air sheet splits.
  • Windway — narrow duct that aims air at the labium.
  • Sch.40 PVC — common plumbing pipe schedule; ID is smaller than the “½″ / ¾″” name on the label.
  • ID — inner diameter of the bore (mm).
  • OD — outer diameter ≈ ID + 2× wall (or stamped on PVC).
  • Embouchure — lip shape + the side mouth hole (bansuri).
  • Cents — 1/100 of a semitone. Tuners show how many cents sharp (+) or flat (−).
  • Equal temperament — Western piano spacing (twelfth-root-of-two). Good enough to place holes; raga intonation is finer.
  • Undercutting — bevelling the inside of a hole to change tone/pitch without moving the mark.
  • Meend — glide; needs flexible embouchure (side-blown) — advanced.
