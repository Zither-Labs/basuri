Swaras (Sa–Ni)

Once you can hold a tone, learn the seven swaras. On the Eastern bansuri, Sa is the top three finger holes covered (nearest the blowing hole), with the lower three open. That is the standard Hindustani teaching map — not “all holes closed.”

Fingering chart (6-hole · Sa = three covered)

Holes left→right = upper (near embouchure) → lower. Filled = closed. “Top” means nearest the blowing hole.

First week: practice Sa–Re–Ga–Ma♯ (three closed → two → one → all open). Add shuddha Ma, middle Pa, komal notes, and notes below Sa when the embouchure is steady.

Two ways to play Pa above Sa (both appear on the bansuriflute.co.uk chart): (1) all six closed with faster air (overblow the mandra-Pa fingering), then open from the bottom for Dha / Ni; (2) 0XXXXX — vent the top hole, close the five below (your teacher’s fingering). Beginners on that site are often told to half-open the top hole first. Always check a clean fifth against the Sa drone.

Komal & tivra (altered swaras)

Shuddha = natural pitch. Two kinds of alteration:

  • Komal (“soft”) — flattened. Used on Re, Ga, Dha, Ni.
  • Tivra (“sharp / intense”) — raised. Used only on Ma.

Sa and Pa never move. Match every altered note to the Sa drone — the ear is the final judge.

Basuri — how to get the sound

Main tool: half-holing. Leave a slit of the hole open (often the lower half of the pad) so the pitch sits between two full fingerings. Roll the finger slowly; tiny changes move pitch a lot.

Also useful on side-blown basuri:

  • Meend — slide from one fingering to the next by gradually uncovering; pass through the komal along the way.
  • Embouchure — roll the flute slightly in/out or soften the air to fine-tune after the finger is close.

Same idea in the upper octave: keep the half-hole, use a bit more focused air for Sa↑ and friends.

Start with komal Re only: drone Sa, play Sa → half-open 3rd hole → full Re → back. When that slot is stable, add komal Ga the same way.

Murali (end-blown) — same idea, less bend

On a recorder-like murali you still half-hole for komal notes and for shuddha Ma. Tivra Ma is still the fully open (or next full) fingering on that tube’s map.

  • The fipple aims the air for you, so embouchure bends are weaker than on basuri — rely more on careful half-cover %.
  • Soft, steady breath; blasting sharpens and can jump octave instead of giving a clean komal.
  • Hole spacing on folk/toy muralis is uneven. Use the murali page notes and tune to the drone, not only to a printed chart.

Ma vs Ma♯ (tivra Ma)

In Indian music, Ma (madhyam) is the only swara that can be raised (sharpened). The others that change are flattened (komal Re, Ga, Dha, Ni). Sa and Pa never move.

  • Shuddha Ma (“plain Ma”) — the natural fourth above Sa (like F if Sa were C). On bansuri it is usually a half-hole on the top finger hole: between Ga (one hole closed) and all-open.
  • Tivra Ma / Ma♯ — the raised fourth (like F♯ if Sa were C). Usual fingering: all holes open. Alternate on the bansuriflute.co.uk chart: top three open, bottom three closed (000XXX). Many ragas (e.g. Yaman) use tivra Ma.

Shop charts that write Ma# mean tivra Ma, not “play a Western sharp key.” If a lesson just says “Ma” without tivra / shuddha, ask which one — or match the raga.

Beginners often learn all-open as the first “Ma” because half-holing is harder. That note is tivra Ma (Ma♯). Add shuddha Ma later with slow half-cover practice.

High register & alternate fingerings

Primary map below follows the 6-hole chart on bansuriflute.co.uk — beginners (Sa = top three closed). Faster, narrower air for higher octaves — not just louder blowing.

Notation

  • ,P ,D ,N or dot below — mandra (lower) saptak
  • S R G M P D N — madhya (middle)
  • S' R' … or one dot above — taar (upper)
  • S'' or two dots above — ati-taar (very high Sa)
  • Code 0 = open · X = closed · left→right = top hole (near blow) → bottom
That site’s tip for high notes: think of a garden hose — same air volume, smaller opening → faster stream. Soft high tone is the goal. Exact top-register response still varies by bamboo.

Other teachers’ high forks (optional)

If your guru’s sheet differs, keep theirs. These also appear in teaching circles:

  • Dha'' · XX000X (close 1–2 and 6 with three open in the middle — close cousin of XXOOOX)
  • Ni'' · X0XXXX or XXXX0X
  • Re' alt. · XX0XXX · Ga' alt. · X00XXX

Tune every option to the drone; keep the fingering that speaks cleanly on your flute.

How to practise

  1. Drone Sa. Climb ,P ,D ,N S R G M♯ with long tones.
  2. Lock madhya Pa both ways: overblown XXXXXX and 0XXXXX (or half-open top).
  3. Continue D N S' with stronger, smaller embouchure.
  4. Add P' (0XXXXX), then D' (XXOOOX), N' (XXOXOO), S'' (XOXOOO).
  5. One new high note per week. Soft tone beats loud squeak.
Murali (end-blown) rarely needs this chart — these fingerings are for side-blown basuri / bansuri.

Practice

  1. Drone Sa on the practice bar; finger Sa (top three closed) until it matches.
  2. Sa → Re → Sa, then add Ga.
  3. All open = Ma♯; return to Sa to check pitch.
  4. Optional: all closed = Pa below Sa — useful, but not your tonic.
  5. When ready: Sa → komal Re (half-open 3rd) → Re; then try komal Ga and shuddha Ma the same slow way.
  6. Later: middle Pa (overblow all-closed or 0XXXXX), then the full high-register chart.

Footnote: Western flute / “C flute” charts

Many beginner web tutorials (especially “C natural middle” shop guides) treat all six holes closed as Sa and open holes upward from the bottom. That matches a Western habit: the lowest tube note is “do” / the named key of the flute.

Eastern classical bansuri teaching usually sets Sa at three holes covered so you also have Pa–Dha–Ni below Sa without changing octave. Same bamboo — different tonic choice.

If your maker labels the flute “C,” that letter often names a Western pitch (sometimes the all-closed note). Still put the practice-bar drone on your three-hole Sa and train the ear to that.
