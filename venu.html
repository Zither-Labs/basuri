Carnatic venu (8-hole flute)

The South Indian classical bamboo flute — also called venu, pullanguzhal, or simply Carnatic / Karnatik flute. It is still side-blown (like bansuri), but the hole count and Sa fingering differ.

Spelling: Carnatic / Karnatic / Karnatik all refer to the same southern classical tradition. This page uses Carnatic + venu.

Eight finger holes — not seven

People often guess “7 holes.” The usual concert venu has eight finger holes in a straight line, plus the blowing hole (mukha randhra).

  • Holes 1–7 — used in normal playing (left 3 fingers + right 4 fingers).
  • Hole 8 — farthest from the lips; usually left open. Used for fine tuning and some special / full-hand fingerings.

So: eight holes on the tube; seven in daily use. That is why “7” and “8” both show up in casual talk.

This room’s main path (swaras, ladder, alankars) uses the Hindustani bansuri map: Sa = top three closed. On venu, Sa = top two closed. Do not mix the charts on the same flute.

Hold & hands

Hold horizontally (usually to your right), slightly down, resting on both thumbs — same family as bansuri posture.

  • Left hand — first three fingers cover holes 1, 2, 3 (nearest the mouth).
  • Right hand — four fingers cover holes 4, 5, 6, 7.
  • Hole 8 — leave open unless a chart asks otherwise.

Seal with finger pads, not tips. Stretch is tighter than a 6-hole bansuri of the same length — smaller / higher keys are easier for small hands.

First sound

Embouchure is the same skill family as basuri first sound: place the mouth hole at the lip edge, gentle airstream across the hole, kiss-and-roll until it speaks.

  1. Open all finger holes (or leave only hole 8’s “open” habit and keep 1–7 free) and find a clear tone.
  2. Close top two → that is your Sa. Match a drone or Listen.
  3. Close top five → basic Pa. Check the fifth against Sa.
  4. Only then add Ri, Ga, Ma with half-holes and teacher charts — Carnatic intonation lives in the cracks.
If you already play bansuri, expect your muscle memory for Sa to be wrong for a week. Relabel: “two closed = Sa” on this flute only.

Beginner swara map (traditional style)

Simplified starting points (hole 8 open). ● = closed, ○ = open. Order = holes 1→8 from the lips.

SwaraHoles 1–8 (● closed)Notes
Sa ● ● ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ ○ Top two closed — tonic
Pa ● ● ● ● ● ○ ○ ○ Top five closed
Ma (traditional) ≈ 6½ closed or cross-finger Often half-hole; “Shashank style” flutes may use six fully closed
Ri / Ga / Da / Ni Half-holes + combinations Use a teacher chart — gamakas need sliding covers

Full chromatic / raga charts vary by school (Palladam / Mali lineage, Shashank-style Ma, etc.). Treat the table above as orientation, not concert gospel.

Practice idea: long tones on Sa and Pa with a drone, then slow Sa–Ri–Ga–Ma–Pa ascending with a printed fingering chart beside you.

Make a venu

Use the flute layout calculator → mode Carnatic venu (8 finger holes).

  • Classic concert size is often near ~14″ long with a modest bore (~¾″ outer class).
  • Eight holes in one line (no offset pancham like Hindustani 7th).
  • Tune Sa (two closed) and Pa (five closed) first; enlarge holes gradually.
  • Hole 8: drill, then leave mostly open — adjust size for overall pitch balance.
